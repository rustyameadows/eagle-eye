# Fieldwork headline review

Frozen discovery pass. I read all 21 clean, full-page screenshots at the supplied CSS widths. I read the 390-pixel views at half their raster width. I revisited the dominant titles and lower editorial headings, with enlarged pair crops for close calls. The packet provides pixels and viewport metadata. Font names, CSS causes and the effects of candidate changes are unverified.

## Findings

1. **“Waterline” opens into “W aterline”.** P009 at 1040, P001 at 820 and P013 at 390. The initial W falls away from the a. The empty wedge below the right arm of the W and the switch to rust at precisely that boundary make the rest of the word read as a separate unit. Reduce the W–a gap while preserving the color treatment. The rest of “aterline” has a coherent rhythm. High confidence.
2. **“Pressed leaves” is excessively letterspaced on the compact page.** P002 at 390. Both words read one letter at a time, particularly the sequence e–s–s–e. P008 at 1040 and P012 at 820 show a much more connected rhythm. Reduce tracking in the compact title. The two-line composition itself is easy to follow. High confidence.
3. **“Night almanac” is crowded throughout.** P020 at 1040, P003 at 820 and P006 at 390. The g–h in “Night” and the a–l–m and a–n–a sequences in “almanac” show how several strokes or feet come very close or run together. The small amount of dark background between adjacent letters makes the whole hero unusually dense relative to the other serif titles. Open tracking throughout, then reread the word space. High confidence.
4. **“Toward open water” opens into “T oward”.** P016 at 1040, P005 at 820 and P018 at 390. The T–o interval interrupts “Toward” before the relatively even “oward”. The o sits well outside the T overhang. Bring the o closer to the T. The spaces between the actual words are legible. High confidence.
5. **“Station index” changes face at the intermediate width.** P010 at 820 has a sans-serif hero with different stroke endings and proportions across both words. P017 at 1040 and P015 at 390 have the same serif display treatment as the journal’s other titles. Restore the matching display face at the intermediate width if the editions share typography. High confidence in the visible mismatch. The intended family and the mechanism require verification.

## Close calls and retained forms

6. **“Weather watch”, W–e.** P021 at 1040, P004 at 820 and P011 at 390. The wide diagonal W leaves a modest pocket before the e, clearest at 1040. I would compare a slightly closer W–e, with low confidence that a change is needed. At normal size the word still reads continuously. The lower “A weather station at the edge of the sound” reads cleanly despite its changing breaks.
7. **“Valley atlas”, V–a.** P014 at 1040, P019 at 820 and P007 at 390. The a tucks under the V more than the surrounding upright letters tuck into one another. There is a narrow, continuous channel of background, and “Valley” reads as a word without a collision. This is the tightest candidate pair for a modest outward trial. I would retain it on original evidence alone. “Away from the ridge” also reads naturally.
8. **“Twelve winds”, T–w.** P020 at 1040, P003 at 820 and P006 at 390. The change from gold to cream emphasizes the initial T; the following w stands just outside its overhang. A closer setting is a possible comparison, with low confidence. The opening has a milder effect than the separation in “Toward” or “Waterline”.
9. **Lower headings and distinct scripts.** No additional optical correction stood out in “The offshore office” and its repeated f forms, “Café des marais” and its accent, the opening and closing quotes in “At slack water”, the letterspaced small caps in “STATION REGISTER”, or the connected Arabic in “دليل المحطات”. The remaining subheads and side notes read coherently at their supplied sizes.
