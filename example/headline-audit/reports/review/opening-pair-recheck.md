# Opening-pair recheck

A fresh agent received the installed skill and three anonymous screenshots at 1040 CSS px and 1× density. The request was “Can you give me a quick read on the typography across these editorial pages? Anything worth a closer look?” The inputs were [page A](../discovery/P020.png), [page B](../discovery/P021.png) and [page C](../discovery/P014.png). Prior findings, source, repaired pages and grading materials were withheld.

## Reviewer response

> I’d start with **“Night almanac.”** The spacing feels tight across the whole title, especially through the `lma` in “almanac,” where the serifs nearly join. I’d try slightly looser tracking on this title alone and reread it at the supplied width.
>
> Two openings are worth a closer look:
>
> - **“Weather watch.”** On a second read, the `W` feels detached from the `e`. The `Va` in “Valley atlas” gives a useful same-size comparison. The `a` sits under the diagonal of the `V`, and “Valley” reads smoothly. I’d try the `e` a little closer to the `W`.
> - **“Twelve winds.”** The gold `T` and the space before the `w` make the initial read separately. Compare it with the cream `T` in “The 03:40 watch” at the same size. I’d keep the gold for the first comparison and tuck the `w` slightly under the crossbar to see whether the word reads more naturally.

## Outcome

The reviewer returned an inline critique and localized both opening pairs of interest with a direction and a visual comparison. Its examples match the display size and have different adjacent letters, so both proposed adjustments still need a controlled trial. This was one discovery review on three selected desktop pages. It produced no preference scores or new source repairs.
