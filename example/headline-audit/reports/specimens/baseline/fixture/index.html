Tidal survey

Toward open water

A tide gauge, six hours of daylight, and a notebook kept at the edge of the North Sound.

Station
North Sound
Observer
Nora Greer
Window
06:00–12:00

Along the tide gauge

At first light, the gauge is all that moves. A ribbon of wrack hangs just below the top bolt and leaves a ruler-straight line on the timber. By the end of the watch the shallow channel under the boardwalk has nearly emptied.

The field book pairs each gauge reading with the direction of the water around the piles. The two accounts agree closely through the first half of the morning.

Instrument note

Gauge zero sits at the base of the southern pile. Heights in this notebook describe the local tide.

“At slack water”

The notebook records a brief pause just after the top of the tide. Silt falls out of the water and the eelgrass turns on its stem.
